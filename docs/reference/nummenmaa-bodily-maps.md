# Nummenmaa and others: bodily maps of emotions

Research behind the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md): where in the body people feel emotions, and how consistent that is. Two papers from the same group in Finland.

## Bodily maps of emotions (2014)

- Lauri Nummenmaa, Enrico Glerean, Riitta Hari and Jari K. Hietanen, "Bodily maps of emotions", *PNAS* 111(2), 646–651, 2014. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1321664111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1321664111). Open access: [PMC3896150](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3896150/).
- **Method:** 701 participants across five experiments. They saw two outlines of a body and painted where they felt activity grow on one and fade on the other, for emotion words, short stories, silent films and faces. Six basic emotions, seven others, and a neutral state.
- **Findings** (summarized from the paper's text, read 2026-11-15; the figures have more detail):
  - most basic emotions showed up in the upper chest, likely breathing and heart rate;
  - the head showed up for every emotion;
  - anger and happiness showed up in the arms and hands;
  - sadness made the limbs fade;
  - disgust showed up in the throat and digestive system;
  - only happiness filled the whole body;
  - fear and sadness looked alike, as did anxiety and depression.
- **Across cultures:** the maps matched between Finnish and Taiwanese participants, and between Finnish and Swedish speakers, so they aren't just figures of speech in one language.

## Maps of subjective feelings (2018)

- Lauri Nummenmaa, Riitta Hari, Jari K. Hietanen and Enrico Glerean, "Maps of subjective feelings", *PNAS* 115(37), 9198–9203, 2018. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1807390115](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1807390115). Open access: [PMC6140475](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6140475/).
- **Method:** 1,026 participants rated 100 feeling states (emotions, thinking, bodily and illness states), judged how alike pairs of them were, and mapped where each was felt.
- **Findings:** the feelings fell into five clusters (positive emotions, negative emotions, cognitive processes, somatic states and illnesses, homeostatic states). How pleasant a feeling is, how much it involves the mind, and how much it's felt in the body best explained how people told feelings apart.
- Taken from the abstract and a summary of it; the full text wasn't read. Worth reading before leaning on it for anything specific.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its figures: no body maps are reproduced.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Places are a sound way in.** Emotions have distinct, fairly consistent body maps, which supports a body wheel organized by where you feel something.
2. **Whole body is a real place.** Happiness filled the whole body and sadness drained the limbs, so feelings that aren't in one spot need a home.
3. **Throat and gut for disgust, chest for most emotions, arms for anger,** which shaped where the rework puts gagging, the chest's many sensations, and tight fists.
4. **Some feelings look alike in the body** (fear and sadness, anxiety and depression), which supports offering two or three feelings per sensation rather than one.
