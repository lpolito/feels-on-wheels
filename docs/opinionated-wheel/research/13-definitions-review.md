## The two approaches tested

Both were added to the Definitions rules as they stood after 2026-11-23. In the judge's report, A1 to A5 are approach A's numbered rules and C1 to C7 the current rules, in order.

These are added to the current Definitions rules. Where they conflict with a current rule, these win.

1. **Don't put the sorting reason on the card.** The card is read by someone holding it up against what they feel, not comparing it with its neighbors. Set a word apart from its siblings by describing how the feeling itself differs, not with a disclaimer about cause, intent, or circumstance whose only job is to separate it from a neighbor. Test: would someone who feels it say this sentence about themselves?
2. **Don't fence the word into one cause or target.** "Only say what's true for most people" has a second direction: list the common situations people use the word for, and the definition must not rule them out. Describe how it seems to the person feeling it, not facts about others' intent or circumstances they may not know.
3. **Land at the word's own strength.** Describe the feeling once it's there, not on the way to it, and neither weaker nor stronger than the word. A word that differs from its parent mainly in strength says what that strength does to you, not just "the parent, but more".
4. **The parent's label may appear, but can't carry the definition.** Cross out the parent's label (or its root) and any near-synonym: what's left must still say what this feeling is. A near-synonym that isn't the same feeling misleads.
5. **The sibling check runs across the whole core, and the core's shared ingredient doesn't count.** Words in one core shouldn't read alike, cousins included. A phrase that restates what the whole family has in common doesn't set a word apart.

Craft: match the label's part of speech as people use it on the wheel ("I feel hurt" is an adjective; "Sympathy" is a noun). Prefer plain words over literary ones. Watch for phrases with a second everyday meaning, and for trailing clauses tacked on to cover more cases.

This is added to the current Definitions rules. Where it conflicts with a current rule, it wins.

**Write each card so someone who feels the word would read it and say "yes, that's it."** Nothing on the card is about the word's neighbors on the wheel, the other person's intent, or what the whole family shares.

### 4. Verdict

**Neither approach alone.** B's one line fixes only the "sorting reason and intent" bucket, which is 3 or 4 of Lucas's notes. His notes fall mostly into other buckets:

- cards fenced to one cause or target: about 10 (Contempt, Hatred, Suspicious, Regret, Unappreciated, Abandoned, Protective, Inferior, Insulted, and Empty)
- near-duplicates inside a core: about 6
- wrong strength or stage: about 4 (Rage, Hopeless, Discouraged, Devastated)
- wording and grammar: about 10 (Vulnerable, Helpless, Disillusioned, Hurt, Sympathy, Misunderstood, Desire, Nostalgic, Lonely, Jealous)

A's text addresses all of these buckets. Its weakness is A4, plus a rule list long enough that the subtler rules weren't applied.

**What earns its place:**

- **A1, folded into C2.** A separate rule isn't needed. Make the sibling check say *how* to set a word apart: by the feeling, not by a disclaimer. That resolves the conflict both writers found between "set it apart" and "don't sort on the card". B's "nothing about the other person's intent" belongs here too, limited to words whose meaning isn't intent (Provoked, Cheated, and Betrayed keep theirs).
- **A2's second direction**, the most valuable rule for Lucas's notes: don't rule out the usual uses. Drop its "describe how it seems, not others' intent" half. That half produced the "as if" and "seem meant" hedges, and the intent line above covers it.
- **A3**, with a fix: the core's card spans everything under it. Without that, writer A failed Happy and Angry for naming a strength.
- **A5**, scoped to the parent and to words in the same core people might confuse, with the shared-ingredient clause. Without a threshold it fired on single shared words (Lost, Restless, Insignificant). Phrase it as the current fit test, which is a threshold: does the card fit the other label as well?
- **From the craft paragraph:** part of speech after "I feel", plain words, second meanings and euphemisms, and tacked-on clauses. Plus A4's last sentence: a near-synonym that names a different feeling misleads.

**What causes harm:**

- **A4's cross-out test.** It conflicts with C4, which says "the parent… is fine", with the rules' own model (Appalled: "Shocked and disgusted"), and with question 3 in rules.md (a child is the parent plus a cause, target, or strength). It produced most of the overreach and the vivid-verb drift. The parent check in C2 catches its real cases (Affection, Horrified, Appalled) without it.
- **B's "what the whole family shares"**, read strictly, bans the parent on any card (B's writer's ambiguity 2). It also bans the pointers the rules ask for elsewhere (Bad's card pointing to Sorry for someone, "Betrayed often comes with anger").
- **The "describe how it seems" half of A2** makes intent words hedge.

**Conflicts with recorded decisions.** These need Lucas, not a rule:

- The rule change reverses rules.md's 2026-11-20 "intent matters" line (Rejected on purpose, Left out maybe by accident). His own notes now ask for it, so update that line in the same change.
- A2 widens Hatred, which Lucas asks for, and Ashamed, which he didn't. Keep Ashamed's self-versus-deed core as the word's meaning (Tangney), not a fence.
- Moved's narrowing is a placement decision, not a card fix.

### Recommended rule text

Two current rules change: replace "Say what sets it apart from its siblings" and "Only say what's true for most people" with the versions below. The last two bullets are new. The rest of current-rules.md stays as written.

> - **Say what sets it apart from the words near it:** its siblings, its parent, and other words in the core someone might mix it up with. Read the definition under each of their labels: if it fits one as well, it needs more. Set it apart by how the feeling differs, not by a clause whose only job is to rule a neighbor out, and leave out the other person's intent unless the word means it. What the whole core shares doesn't set a word apart. *"Hurt by how someone treated you" fits Betrayed, Rejected, and Unappreciated alike.*
> - **Only say what's true for most people who feel it, and don't leave out what most people mean by it.** Think of the usual situations and targets people use the word for; the card shouldn't rule one out, unless the word's place on the wheel narrows it on purpose (see two places). Don't tell one person's story. *Pressured: Pushed by demands or expectations from others or yourself*, not "Others push you harder than you can go".
> - **Match the word's strength and stage.** Describe the feeling once it's there, not on the way to it, and no milder or stronger than people mean it. When a word is mostly a stronger form of its parent, say what that strength does to you. A core's card covers everything under it, mild to strong.
> - **Read it after "I feel".** The card fits how the label is used ("I feel proud" wants a description, "I feel awe" a noun). Use plain everyday words over metaphors and idioms, and avoid words with a second everyday meaning or a euphemism. Don't lean on a near-synonym that names a different feeling. Keep it to the feeling; don't tack on clauses to cover more cases.

None of the examples come from Lucas's notes. The Hurt and Pressured examples are the current rules' own. One more line is optional, a process step rather than a rule: "After rewriting several cards, read them together, and vary any opening or ending that repeats." Writer A's self-check shows it works, but it's only needed if drift appears.

I don't recommend a length rule. Lucas's "a smidge too short" is mostly covered by the parent check and the strength rule. A length target invites padding, which he rejected.

### 5. What no rule fixes

These need a decision, research, or Lucas's taste:

1. **Mortified and Cringe:** whether Cringe stays as a label beside Mortified, given the everyday use for your own memories. A label decision.
2. **Sorry for someone:** whether there's a single word. Pity reads condescending, and Sympathy is already under Love. Without "even from a distance", the two are one feeling in two cores. Placement and label decision.
3. **Empty under Grief:** it's narrowed to loss on purpose, and the general sense lives under Numb. Lucas may not have seen that. Confirm the two-places choice, or make the Numb card plainly the general one.
4. **On edge:** "braced" versus "expecting something to go wrong", and whether to add a word for expecting it (apprehensive). A word-list decision.
5. **Discouraged:** whether it's "wanting to give up" (Lucas) or milder (usage). Research or taste.
6. **Jealous:** keeping rival-threat jealousy (the classic sense) while separating it from envy, and Possessive's place under it. Needs a decision on what Jealous's card covers.
