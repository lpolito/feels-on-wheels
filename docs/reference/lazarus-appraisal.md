# Richard Lazarus: what happened, and stress

The guides in the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md)'s rules for which core a word belongs to, and why Stressed isn't a word inside a core.

## What it is

- Richard S. Lazarus, *Emotion and Adaptation*, Oxford University Press, 1991: each emotion has a "core relational theme", the kind of thing that happened. Anger's is "a demeaning offense against me and mine"; love's is desiring or sharing affection. There's no theme for surprise, or for a catch-all.
- Richard S. Lazarus, "From psychological stress to the emotions: A history of changing outlooks", *Annual Review of Psychology* 44, 1–21, 1993. [doi:10.1146/annurev.ps.44.020193.000245](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ps.44.020193.000245): knowing someone feels angry, anxious, guilty, or sad "tells us much more" than knowing they're stressed.
- The 1993 article was read in full; the 1991 book's themes come from secondary summaries.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Each core has a what-happened guide,** adapted from appraisal theory, for placing a word. It isn't how people choose: they tap by what they feel.
2. **Stress is an umbrella,** so the catch-all's words are specific feelings instead.
