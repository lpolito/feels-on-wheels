# June Tangney: shame, guilt, and embarrassment

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) keeps Ashamed, Guilty, and Embarrassed apart, and near each other.

## What it is

- June Price Tangney, Jeff Stuewig and Debra J. Mashek, "Moral emotions and moral behavior", *Annual Review of Psychology* 58, 345–372, 2007. [doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145).
- **The idea:** shame is about the self, guilt about something you did. Guilt pulls toward repair. Embarrassment is less about morality, comes from minor public mishaps, and is the mildest and shortest of the three (Tangney and others, 1996).
- The 2007 review was read in full; the 1996 finding about embarrassment comes from a textbook summary.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Three different feelings, side by side:** Ashamed ends Sad, and Guilty and Embarrassed begin Bad, beside it.
2. **Shame doesn't always make you hide** (see [Leach and Cidam](leach-cidam-shame.md)), so Ashamed's card doesn't say it does.
