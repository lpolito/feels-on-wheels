# Hayes and others: experiential avoidance

The research behind the urges in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md) that pull away from a feeling itself, such as wanting to numb out.

## What it is

- Steven C. Hayes, Kelly G. Wilson, Elizabeth V. Gifford, Victoria M. Follette and Kirk Strosahl, "Experiential avoidance and behavioral disorders: A functional dimensional approach to diagnosis and treatment", *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 64(6), 1152–1168, 1996. [doi:10.1037/0022-006X.64.6.1152](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006X.64.6.1152).
- From the group that developed acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT).
- **The idea:** experiential avoidance is being unwilling to stay in contact with your own feelings, thoughts and body sensations, and acting to escape or change them. The paper argues that many problems, substance use among them, can be understood as costly efforts to escape or avoid emotions.
- Taken from the abstract and secondary summaries, read 2026-11-16; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Urges are often a way to get away from something, the feeling itself included.** Running from a threat gets away from what's happening; numbing out, bingeing or sleeping it off gets away from the feeling. Both are urges a person might not recognize for what they are.
2. **Acceptance is its opposite.** In acceptance and commitment therapy, acceptance means being willing to have your feelings and your situation as they are instead of fighting them ([ACBS](https://contextualscience.org/book/export/html/10), read 2026-11-16). That's the "not fighting reality" reading of Nothing much.
3. **These urges often come with hard feelings that are hard to name,** which is when someone reaches for the body wheel.
