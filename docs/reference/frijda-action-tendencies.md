# Nico Frijda: action tendencies

The research behind the body wheel's **Urges** area in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md): an emotion comes with a pull toward doing something.

## What it is

- Nico H. Frijda (1927–2015), a Dutch psychologist at the University of Amsterdam.
- *The Emotions*, Cambridge University Press, 1986: the book that sets out emotions as states of **action readiness**, a readiness to act in a certain way toward what's going on.
- Tested in: Nico H. Frijda, Peter Kuipers and Elisabeth ter Schure, "Relations among emotion, appraisal, and emotional action readiness", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 57(2), 212–228, 1989.
- Later built on by the GRID study of emotion words across languages (Fontaine, Scherer and Soriano, eds., *Components of Emotional Meaning*, Oxford University Press, 2013), whose chapter on action tendencies is summarized at <https://www.unige.ch/cisa/research/current-specific-research-projects/language-and-culture/grid-book/chapter-11-emotion-doing-action-tendency-component>.

## The kinds of urge

Frijda's list pairs kinds of action readiness with emotions, for example approaching with desire, avoiding with fear, rejecting with disgust, fighting with anger, being with someone with enjoyment or trust, attending with interest, and being interrupted with surprise. The exact list differs between his book and later work; these were taken from secondary summaries on 2026-11-15, not from the book itself.

Frijda also wrote on revenge: "The lex talionis: On vengeance", in Stephanie H. M. van Goozen, Nanne E. van de Poll and Joseph A. Sergeant, eds., *Emotions: Essays on Emotion Theory*, Erlbaum, 1994. He treats the urge for vengeance as strong and hard to limit, and as ending once the offender has suffered in a comparable way. Taken from the abstract and citing papers, read 2026-11-16.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not a word list.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Urges are part of an emotion, not a stand-in for one,** so a wheel area for them is a fair way in, especially for people who notice the pull before the feeling.
2. **The kinds of urge cover most core feelings,** not only the strong negative ones. The body wheel leaves out Surprised's (attending) on purpose.
3. **Urges are different from sensations,** so they get their own area instead of being mixed in with the places.
