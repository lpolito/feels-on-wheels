# Maier and Seligman: learned helplessness

The research behind the body wheel's **Give up** in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md).

## What it is

- Steven F. Maier and Martin E. P. Seligman, "Learned helplessness at fifty: Insights from neuroscience", *Psychological Review* 123(4), 349–367, 2016. [doi:10.1037/rev0000033](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000033). Open access: [PMC4920136](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920136/).
- The authors' review of fifty years of their research, which began with Seligman and Maier's experiments in 1967.
- **The idea:** after bad things they can't control, animals and people stop trying to escape or change them, even once they could. The 2016 review argues that giving up is the default response to what you can't control, and that what's learned is the sense of control that undoes it.
- Mostly animal research; studies in people are older and separate. Taken from the abstract and secondary summaries, read 2026-11-16; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Giving up is a real response,** not just a turn of phrase, to what you can't control. Pairing it with Hopeless, Inadequate, and Helpless, the feeling the research is named for, is the body wheel's choice, not the review's finding.
