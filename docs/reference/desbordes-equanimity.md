# Desbordes and others: equanimity isn't indifference

The research behind the body wheel's **Nothing much**, a change made after the [body wheel rework spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md) was approved (see its [changes](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-changes.md)): calm acceptance and numbness can be hard to tell apart.

## What it is

- Gaëlle Desbordes, Tim Gard, Elizabeth A. Hoge, Britta K. Hölzel, Catherine Kerr, Sara W. Lazar, Andrew Olendzki and David R. Vago, "Moving beyond mindfulness: Defining equanimity as an outcome measure in meditation and contemplative research", *Mindfulness* 6(2), 2015. [doi:10.1007/s12671-013-0269-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-013-0269-8). Open access: [PMC4350240](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/).
- **The idea:** equanimity is meeting pleasant and unpleasant experiences with the same evenness. The paper says it is easily confused with indifference or apathy, since both can quiet an emotional response. The difference is that with equanimity you still care and stay interested. Equanimity isn't the absence of feeling either: a blunted response where one would be expected is a warning sign, not calm.
- Read in its open-access text, as summarized by a fetch tool, on 2026-11-16 (the first half of the paper).

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Not caring and being content can feel alike from the inside,** which is exactly what Nothing much offers: one sensation, two readings (Content and Peaceful, or Apathetic).
2. **Whether you still care is the tell,** and it's how the wheel defines Apathetic ("Not caring or feeling much about anything"). A definition or a card for these words could someday say so; that's for the opinionated feelings wheel.
