## Adding a source

- Add one file per source: what it is, author, date, link, usage terms if stated, what we take from it, and anything unverified marked as such.
- Add it to the table above and to [Credits](#credits) below.
- These are research notes, not app content; the [content rules](../../src/data/README.md) decide what ships. Notes describe a source and may quote a few words as examples. They never transcribe its word list or layout.

## Credits

Every source the app's content draws on, credited here and in the root README's [Credits](../../README.md#credits), even where no license requires it. The app's About page (`page.about` in `src/data/locales/en.json`) names only the main ones and links to the README's Credits. Attributions were confirmed on 2026-11-09; see each note.

- **Gloria Willcox**, "The Feeling Wheel", *Transactional Analysis Journal* 12(4), 1982: the original feelings wheel.
- **Geoffrey Roberts**, "I Feel – Emotional Word Wheel – The Feel Wheel", imgur, 2015: the seven-core wheel the first version's words were based on.
- **Lindsay Braman**, "Emotion-Sensation Wheel", 2020, lindsaybraman.com: the idea of a feelings wheel that includes body sensations.
- **feelingswheel.com**, the interactive site whose tap-to-trace interaction inspired ours.
- **Nummenmaa, Glerean, Hari and Hietanen**, "Bodily maps of emotions", *PNAS*, 2014, and "Maps of subjective feelings", *PNAS*, 2018 ([note](nummenmaa-bodily-maps.md)): where in the body people feel emotions.
- **Nico Frijda**, *The Emotions*, 1986 ([note](frijda-action-tendencies.md)): emotions as a readiness to act, behind the body wheel's Urges.
- **A. D. Craig**, "How do you feel? Interoception: the sense of the physiological condition of the body", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2002 ([note](craig-interoception.md)): a sensation as the body sensed from inside.
- **Marsha Linehan**, *DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets*, 2nd ed., 2015 ([note](linehan-dbt-model-of-emotions.md)): body sensations and action urges as parts of an emotion.
- **Hayes, Wilson, Gifford, Follette and Strosahl**, "Experiential avoidance and behavioral disorders", *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 1996 ([note](hayes-experiential-avoidance.md)): urges to get away from a feeling.
- **G. Alan Marlatt**, urge surfing, from *Relapse Prevention*, 1985 ([note](marlatt-urge-surfing.md)): noticing an urge.
- **Paul Rozin and April Fallon**, "A perspective on disgust", *Psychological Review*, 1987 ([note](rozin-disgust.md)): disgust as rejecting something from the mouth.
- **Zoltán Kövecses**, *Metaphor and Emotion*, 2000 ([note](kovecses-emotion-metaphors.md)): idioms that come from the body.
- **American Psychiatric Association**, *DSM-5*, 2013 ([note](dsm-5-symptoms.md)): the body symptoms of panic, anxiety and depression.
- **Ad Vingerhoets**, *Why Only Humans Weep*, 2013 ([note](vingerhoets-crying.md)): why people cry.
- **Eisenberger, Lieberman and Williams**, "Does rejection hurt?", *Science*, 2003 ([note](eisenberger-social-pain.md)): rejection as pain.
- **Duguid and Goncalo**, "Living large", *Psychological Science*, 2012 ([note](duguid-goncalo-power-height.md)): feeling powerful makes you feel taller.
- **Goetz, Keltner and Simon-Thomas**, "Compassion", *Psychological Bulletin*, 2010 ([note](goetz-compassion.md)): the urge to help.
- **Mikulincer and Shaver**, *Attachment in Adulthood*, 2007 ([note](mikulincer-shaver-attachment.md)): insecurity and wanting closeness.
- **Maier and Seligman**, "Learned helplessness at fifty", *Psychological Review*, 2016 ([note](maier-seligman-learned-helplessness.md)): giving up after what you can't control.
- **Leslie Greenberg**, *Emotion-Focused Therapy*, 2002 ([note](greenberg-emotion-focused-therapy.md)): anger that covers hurt or fear.
- **James Russell**, "A circumplex model of affect", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1980 ([note](russell-circumplex.md)): pleasant and unpleasant low-energy feelings.
- **Desbordes and others**, "Moving beyond mindfulness", *Mindfulness*, 2015 ([note](desbordes-equanimity.md)): equanimity isn't indifference.
- **Paul Rozin, Laura Lowery, Sumio Imada and Jonathan Haidt**, "The CAD triad hypothesis", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1999 ([note](rozin-disgust.md)): anger at violated rights, disgust at what's degrading.
- **Shaver, Schwartz, Kirson and O'Connor**, "Emotion knowledge", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1987, with its replications ([note](shaver-emotion-prototypes.md)): the families people sort feeling words into.
- **James Russell and Beverley Fehr**, "Fuzzy concepts in a fuzzy hierarchy", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1994 ([note](russell-fehr-fuzzy-categories.md)): feeling words belong to a family by degree.
- **Alan Cowen and Dacher Keltner**, "Self-report captures 27 distinct categories of emotion bridged by continuous gradients", *PNAS*, 2017 ([note](cowen-keltner-emotion-categories.md)): feelings shade into each other.
- **Jackson and others**, "Emotion semantics show both cultural variation and universal structure", *Science*, 2019 ([note](jackson-emotion-semantics.md)): pleasant or unpleasant is the first split across languages.
- **Ortony, Clore and Foss**, "The referential structure of the affective lexicon", *Cognitive Science*, 1987, and "The psychological foundations of the affective lexicon", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1987 ([note](ortony-clore-foss-affective-lexicon.md)): words for what happened to you, and body states.
- **Richard Lazarus**, *Emotion and Adaptation*, 1991, and "From psychological stress to the emotions", *Annual Review of Psychology*, 1993 ([note](lazarus-appraisal.md)): what happened, for each feeling; stress as an umbrella.
- **Danckert, Mugon, Struk and Eastwood**, "Boredom: What is it good for?", 2018 ([note](danckert-boredom.md)): boredom as an emotion.
- **Tangney, Stuewig and Mashek**, "Moral emotions and moral behavior", *Annual Review of Psychology*, 2007 ([note](tangney-shame-guilt.md)): shame, guilt, and embarrassment.
- **Colin Wayne Leach and Atilla Cidam**, "When is shame linked to constructive approach orientation?", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2015 ([note](leach-cidam-shame.md)): shame and repair.
- **Zickfeld and others**, "Kama muta", *Emotion*, 2019 ([note](zickfeld-kama-muta.md)): being moved.
- **Fontaine, Scherer, Roesch and Ellsworth**, "The world of emotions is not two-dimensional", *Psychological Science*, 2007 ([note](fontaine-emotion-dimensions.md)): what separates anger from fear.
- **Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez and Pollak**, "Emotional expressions reconsidered", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 2019 ([note](barrett-facial-expressions.md)): faces don't reliably show feelings.
- **Debra Trampe, Jordi Quoidbach and Maxime Taquet**, "Emotions in everyday life", *PLOS ONE*, 2015 ([note](trampe-daily-emotions.md)): feelings reported together.
- **Barbra Zupan, Lynn Dempsey and Katelyn Hartwell**, "Categorising emotion words", *Language and Cognition*, 2023 ([note](zupan-categorising-emotion-words.md)): which category people pick for a word.
- **Sara Algoe**, "Find, remind, and bind", *Social and Personality Psychology Compass*, 2012, and **Algoe and Haidt**, "Witnessing excellence in action", *The Journal of Positive Psychology*, 2009 ([note](algoe-gratitude.md)): gratitude.
- **Nook and others**, "Charting the development of emotion comprehension and abstraction", *Emotion*, 2020 ([note](nook-emotion-definitions.md)): how people define feeling words.
- **Lieberman, Inagaki, Tabibnia and Crockett**, "Subjective responses to emotional stimuli during labeling, reappraisal, and distraction", *Emotion*, 2011 ([note](lieberman-affect-labeling.md)): naming a feeling.
- **Saif M. Mohammad**, NRC VAD Lexicon v2.1, 2025 ([note](nrc-vad-lexicon.md)): how pleasant a word is, used as a check while choosing words.
- **Warriner, Kuperman and Brysbaert**, "Norms of valence, arousal, and dominance for 13,915 English lemmas", *Behavior Research Methods*, 2013 ([note](warriner-affective-norms.md)): how pleasant a word is.
- **Scott, Keitel, Becirspahic, Yao and Sereno**, "The Glasgow Norms", *Behavior Research Methods*, 2019 ([note](glasgow-norms.md)): words with more than one meaning.
- **Baron-Cohen, Golan, Wheelwright, Granader and Hill**, "Emotion word comprehension from 4 to 16 years old", *Frontiers in Evolutionary Neuroscience*, 2010 ([note](baron-cohen-emotion-vocabulary.md)): which feeling words children know.
- **De Deyne, Navarro, Perfors, Brysbaert and Storms**, "The 'Small World of Words' English word association norms", *Behavior Research Methods*, 2019 ([note](small-world-of-words.md)): which words people link.
- **Google Books Ngram** ([note](google-books-ngram.md)): how often people write a phrase.
