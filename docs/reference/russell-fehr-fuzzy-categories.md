# Russell and Fehr: fuzzy categories of feelings

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) asks whether a word fits its parent better than anywhere else, not whether it fits perfectly.

## What it is

- James A. Russell and Beverley Fehr, "Fuzzy concepts in a fuzzy hierarchy: Varieties of anger", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 67(2), 186–205, 1994. [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.67.2.186](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.2.186).
- **The idea:** feeling words are fuzzy categories. Some words are better examples of anger than others, and the kinds of anger (jealousy among them) belong to it only by degree. A later paper describes disgust, too, as appearing as a subcategory of anger across their free listing, typicality ratings, and reaction times; the abstract itself names jealousy, fury, and annoyance.
- Taken from the abstract and a later paper's description; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Membership is a matter of degree,** so a word goes where it fits best, and a card can name its second home.
2. **An absolute test fails real words:** "the parent's meaning is true of every word under it" was dropped for this reason.
