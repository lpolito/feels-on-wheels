# A. D. Craig: interoception

The research behind the body wheel's test for a sensation in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md): a sensation is the body sensed from inside.

## What it is

- A. D. (Bud) Craig, "How do you feel? Interoception: the sense of the physiological condition of the body", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 3(8), 655–666, 2002. [doi:10.1038/nrn894](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn894). [PubMed 12154366](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12154366/).
- Followed by A. D. Craig, "How do you feel — now? The anterior insula and human awareness", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 10(1), 59–70, 2009.
- **The idea:** interoception is the sense of the body's own condition: heartbeat, breathing, the gut, temperature, muscle tension, pain, hunger and the like. Craig describes one system that carries all of it to the brain and argues it underlies feelings and emotion.
- Taken from the abstract and secondary summaries, read 2026-11-16; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text or figures.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **A sensation is sensed from inside.** Interoception (and proprioception, the sense of where your body is and how it's held) is what you'd still notice with your eyes closed and nobody watching. That's where the body wheel's test comes from; the wording of the test is ours.
2. **What others see isn't interoception.** A smile or raised eyebrows is an expression seen from outside, so it fails the test, while the felt side of it (a hot face, clenched teeth) passes.
