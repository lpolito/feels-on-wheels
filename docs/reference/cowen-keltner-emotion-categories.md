# Cowen and Keltner: 27 kinds of feeling, joined by gradients

Evidence in the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) that feelings shade into each other.

## What it is

- Alan S. Cowen and Dacher Keltner, "Self-report captures 27 distinct categories of emotion bridged by continuous gradients", *PNAS* 114(38), E7900–E7909, 2017. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1702247114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1702247114).
- **What they did:** 853 people in the United States rated a sample of 2,185 short film clips (30 each in most of the surveys) for what they felt.
- **What they found:** 27 kinds of reported experience, joined by gradients rather than sharp edges (anxiety shades into fear, fear into horror). Confusion and interest are among them.
- **A critique:** Barrett and colleagues (2018) argued the analysis leaned on a fixed word list and averaged away individual differences; Cowen and Keltner replied. Both sides accept fuzzy edges.
- Taken from the abstract and the published exchange; the full paper wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Categories shade into each other,** so some words could sit under two parents.
2. **Confused and Curious count as feelings,** though some theories call them states of mind.
3. **It doesn't say how to nest words:** its categories don't form a tree, and it measures reactions to clips, not looking for a word.
