## 5. A plain-language rule

> **Should avoiding idioms be a rule? Do we have rules around simple language?**

There is a rule now, but it's loose. `rules.md` says "Use plain everyday words over metaphors and idioms", which is a preference rather than a rule. `ACCESSIBILITY.md` says cards are "short, everyday words", and `src/data/README.md` says "short, plain-language prose". None of them says what plain means, so idioms like *pushing your buttons* and *out of sorts* got through.

I'd make it a real rule. Idioms are among the hardest things to read for people learning English, for people who take words literally (many autistic people do), and for anyone reading while upset. The last group is everyone using this app. Accessibility guidelines say the same thing: WCAG's *Unusual Words* criterion names idioms, and W3C's guidance for people with cognitive disabilities asks for literal language. Neither has a note in `docs/reference/` yet; Part 3 adds them when the rule moves into `src/data/README.md`.

The rule shouldn't ban every comparison. The body wheel's rule (`src/data/README.md`, Idioms) already draws a line that works here too. A phrase that names something you really feel is fine, like *butterflies*. A phrase that only stands for a feeling isn't, like *cold feet*. For cards, that means *heavy*, *hollow*, *stung*, and *heated* stay: they say how the feeling feels, and they grew out of what the body does (Kövecses). *Pushing your buttons*, *out of sorts*, and *gets on your nerves* go: you can't work out what they mean from their words.

Replace the "plain everyday words" sentence in `rules.md`, Definitions, with this bullet:

> **Write it so anyone gets it on the first read,** including someone learning English, someone who reads words literally, and someone too upset to puzzle over a sentence.
> - **Common words.** *Longing*, not *wistful*.
> - **No idioms whose meaning you can't work out from their words** (*pushing your buttons*, *out of sorts*, *ill at ease*). A comparison that says how the feeling feels is fine, as on the body wheel: *heavy*, *hollow*, *stung*, *as if your emotions have been switched off*.
> - **One sentence, with no colons or semicolons.** When a second half could attach to the wrong word, repeat the small word that starts it (*about*, *that*, *whether*) or add a comma. Keep the parts of a list the same kind (*a wish to undo it*, not *wishing you could undo it* after a noun).
> - **Present tense** for the feeling, and contractions, as the other cards use.

`ACCESSIBILITY.md`'s "Plain language" line would point to it. *Lucas, 2026-11-25* gets a dated line, as with his earlier decisions.

## 8. Nesting

> **I just want to make sure the nesting on these cards still make sense.**

I checked every parent whose card or whose children's cards changed. A parent's card has to hold for each word under it ("the parent's meaning has to hold for every child", [learnings](../learnings.md#traps-in-choosing-words)). Most still fit. Three don't, and they're shown first.

### Three fixes

#### Affection, under Love, over Tenderness, Protective, and Cared for

- **Approved:** A warm fondness that makes you glad to have someone or something around.
- **Proposed:** A warm fondness you feel for someone or something, or that someone shows you.
- **Why:** Cared for is about receiving warmth, but Affection's card is only about giving it, both now and as approved. Someone who feels cared for and taps Love → Affection should see a card that fits them. The other two children, Tenderness and Protective, are about giving, and the first half still covers them.

#### Moved, under Compassion

- **Now:** Stirred deeply, maybe to tears, by someone's suffering or courage.
- **Proposed:** Deeply affected, maybe to tears, by someone's suffering or their courage through it.
- **Why:** Compassion's card is "Feeling for someone who's suffering, with a wish to help". Being moved by courage on its own doesn't sit under it, but being moved by courage in the face of suffering does. The new card also loses the idiom *stirred* (part 7).

#### Scared (core), over Anxious, Nervous, Terrified, Shocked, and Vulnerable

- **Approved:** Afraid that something bad could happen to you or to someone or something you care about.
- **Proposed:** Afraid or shaken, as if something bad could happen to you or to someone or something you care about.
- **Why:** Shocked (with Startled and Stunned under it) is about something that already happened, so "afraid that something could happen" missed it. "Shaken" is the word Shocked's own card uses, and a parent can use its child's words.

### Parents that still fit

- **Love:** warmth, a wish to be close, and being cared about cover all four middle words. Affection and Compassion are warmth, Connected is closeness, and Attracted is the wish to be close.
- **Connected** ("Close to someone, or part of something bigger") over **Understood, Valued, and Accepted.** All three are about being received by others, which is a kind of closeness.
- **Compassion** over **Sympathy:** both are about someone having a hard time.
- **Attracted** over **Desire and Aroused:** both are the romantic or sexual pull the card names.
- **Jealous** over **Envious** (someone has what you want) and **Possessive** (could take away someone or something you care about). Each half of Jealous's card is one child.
- **Offended** ("words or actions that feel disrespectful") over **Insulted** (a low opinion of you) and **Indignant.** A low opinion is a kind of disrespect.
- **Vulnerable** ("Unprotected…") over **Helpless, Exposed, and Threatened:** each is a way of being unprotected.
- **Grief** ("Deep sorrow after losing someone or something you love") over **Heartbroken.** The approved card adds "something you had high hopes for", which reaches a little past Grief, since a lost final isn't something you loved. I'd still keep it there. Most heartbreak is a love lost, and that's who taps Grief. Someone heartbroken over a lost game would more likely tap Disappointed, and Disappointed's card fits them at a milder strength. Disappointed already has three children, so Heartbroken can't go there too.
- **Annoyed** over **Irritable, Impatient, and Provoked**; **Frustrated** over **Trapped and Fed up**; **Anxious** over **Worried**; **Down** over **Defeated**; **Grief** over **Devastated**; **Disappointed** over **Disillusioned**; **Hopeful** over **Encouraged**; **Proud** over **Triumphant**; **Excited** over **Exhilarated**: each child is still a kind or a strength of its parent.
- **Angry, Happy, Sad, and Bad:** each new core card still covers the middle words under it, from mild to strong.
