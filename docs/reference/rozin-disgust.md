# Paul Rozin: disgust as rejecting something from the mouth

The research behind where disgust sits in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md): the mouth and throat.

## What it is

- Paul Rozin and April E. Fallon, "A perspective on disgust", *Psychological Review* 94(1), 23–41, 1987. [doi:10.1037/0033-295X.94.1.23](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.94.1.23).
- Later reviewed in Paul Rozin, Jonathan Haidt and Clark McCauley, "Disgust", in *Handbook of Emotions*, Guilford Press (several editions).
- **The idea:** disgust started as revulsion at the thought of taking something offensive into the mouth. Its face (pushing out of the mouth, closing the nose) and its body signs (nausea, gagging) are about rejecting food. Later it spread to other people and to what's morally wrong.
- Taken from the abstract and secondary summaries, read 2026-11-16; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Disgust is felt in the mouth, throat and stomach,** which agrees with [Nummenmaa's bodily maps](nummenmaa-bodily-maps.md) and puts disgust's sensations in Throat / mouth and Stomach.
2. **Nausea is disgust's own sensation,** felt from inside, which is why the body wheel offers Queasy for it. Gagging is closer to the act of rejecting something than to a feeling you'd notice, and someone gagging usually knows why, so the body wheel leaves it out.
