# Lieberman and others: naming a feeling

What the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) does and doesn't claim about why finding the word helps.

## What it is

- Matthew D. Lieberman, Tristen K. Inagaki, Golnaz Tabibnia and Molly J. Crockett, "Subjective responses to emotional stimuli during labeling, reappraisal, and distraction", *Emotion* 11(3), 468–480, 2011. [doi:10.1037/a0023503](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023503).
- **What they found:** in four studies, labeling a feeling lowered self-reported distress a little every time, and lowered pleasure from pleasant images too. People predicted labeling would make them feel worse.
- **Related:** telling unpleasant feelings apart goes with slightly better coping (r ≈ −.15), mostly in correlational studies, and two small training trials had mixed results.
- Read through Europe PMC's full-text feed during the opinionated wheel's research. The findings come mostly from one lab.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **No rule rests on "more precise words help".** No study shows feelings wheels help; the wheel's case is that it helps people find a word.
