# Nook and others: how people define feeling words

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md)'s definition rules don't forbid a card from naming what usually causes a feeling.

## What it is

- Erik C. Nook, Caitlin M. Stavish, Stephanie F. Sasse, Hilary K. Lambert, Patrick Mair, Katie A. McLaughlin and Leah H. Somerville, "Charting the development of emotion comprehension and abstraction from childhood to adulthood using observer-rated and linguistic measures", *Emotion* 20(5), 773–792, 2020. [doi:10.1037/emo0000609](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000609).
- **What they found:** asked to define feeling words, children lean on concrete situations, and adults' definitions grow more abstract, but adults still often define a feeling by what typically causes it.
- Cited during the opinionated wheel's work on definitions (2026-11-23); the paper's details here are from its abstract (196 people aged 4 to 25, defining 24 feeling words), checked on 2026-11-26; the full text wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **A card can say what usually causes a feeling,** as long as it describes the feeling and doesn't tell one person's story.
