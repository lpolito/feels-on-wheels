# James Russell: the circumplex model of affect

The research behind the body wheel's **Nothing much**, a change made after the [body wheel rework spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md) was approved (see its [changes](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-changes.md)): a quiet, low-energy feeling can be pleasant or unpleasant.

## What it is

- James A. Russell, "A circumplex model of affect", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 39(6), 1161–1178, 1980. [doi:10.1037/h0077714](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077714).
- **The idea:** feelings sit on a circle around two dimensions, how pleasant they are (valence) and how activated (arousal). Relaxed and calm feelings are low in arousal and pleasant; tired, sleepy, and depressed ones are low in arousal and unpleasant.
- Taken from secondary summaries and course material, such as [Penn State's chapter on dimensional models](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/circumplex-models/), read 2026-11-16; the paper wasn't read in full.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text or figure.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Low arousal doesn't say how it feels.** A quiet body with not much going on can be contentment or not caring. Valence, not the body, tells them apart, which is why Nothing much offers Content and At peace as well as Unmotivated.
