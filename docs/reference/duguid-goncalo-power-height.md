# Duguid and Goncalo: feeling powerful makes you feel taller

The research behind the body wheel's **Feeling tall** in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md).

## What it is

- Michelle M. Duguid and Jack A. Goncalo, "Living large: The powerful overestimate their own height", *Psychological Science* 23(1), 2012. [doi:10.1177/0956797611422915](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611422915).
- **The idea:** across three experiments with 266 people, those made to feel powerful judged themselves taller than they are: against a pole, in self-reported height, and in the height of an avatar they chose.
- Taken from press releases and secondary coverage, including [Cornell's news story](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2012/01/powerful-people-overestimate-their-height), read 2026-11-16; the paper wasn't read. The authors described it as a first step, and it hasn't been checked whether it has been replicated.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Power changes how big you feel.** The study made people feel powerful and measured how tall they judged themselves, not a sensation. Reading that as "feeling tall", and extending it to confidence and courage, is our step, not the study's.
2. **An early finding,** so it backs the word lightly. Common speech ("walking tall", "ten feet tall") backs it too.
