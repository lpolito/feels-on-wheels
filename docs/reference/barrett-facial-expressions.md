# Barrett and others: faces don't reliably show feelings

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) doesn't keep the old cores just because they match the basic emotions read from faces.

## What it is

- Lisa Feldman Barrett, Ralph Adolphs, Stacy Marsella, Aleix M. Martinez and Seth D. Pollak, "Emotional expressions reconsidered: Challenges to inferring emotion from human facial movements", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 20(1), 1–68, 2019. [doi:10.1177/1529100619832930](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100619832930).
- **What they found:** facial movements aren't reliable or specific signs of emotions; for example, people scowl in only about a quarter of angry moments (Barrett's own figure in coverage of the review; another summary gives about 30%).
- **Background:** the usual feelings wheel's cores (besides Bad) match Paul Ekman's basic emotions, chosen largely because faces showing them were recognized across cultures.
- Taken from the publisher's summary and press coverage; the full review wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **A list of facial signals isn't a list of words people use about themselves,** so it isn't a reason to keep the old cores.
