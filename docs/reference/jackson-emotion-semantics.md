# Jackson and others: emotion words across 2,474 languages

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) splits pleasant from unpleasant first, and why translations need care.

## What it is

- Joshua Conrad Jackson, Joseph Watts, Teague R. Henry, Johann-Mattis List, Robert Forkel, Peter J. Mucha, Simon J. Greenhill, Russell D. Gray and Kristen A. Lindquist, "Emotion semantics show both cultural variation and universal structure", *Science* 366(6472), 1517–1522, 2019. [doi:10.1126/science.aaw8160](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaw8160).
- **What they did:** looked at which emotion meanings share one word (colexification) across 2,474 languages.
- **What they found:** emotion meanings vary across languages more than color words do, and the variation follows geography. Pleasantness was the strongest predictor of which meanings go together in most language families, and energy in the rest. "Anxiety" goes with fear in some families and with grief and regret in others.
- Taken from the paper's PDF, press summaries, and the authors' repository record. The count of families where pleasantness came first ("13 of 19") is from secondary sources.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Pleasant or unpleasant is the first split,** across languages too.
2. **English's details don't carry over:** English Mad and Angry are both *enojado* in Spanish. A translation needs a check for two positions that collapse into one word.
