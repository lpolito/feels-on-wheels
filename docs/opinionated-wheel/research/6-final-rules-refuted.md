# Refutation of the final rule set (rules.md, 2026-11-20)

(Written on 2026-11-21 by a fresh agent given only `rules.md` and research reports 1 to 5, not the learnings or the wheel; saved here by the main session because the agent couldn't write files. Its scripts and the Warriner download were in the session scratchpad and are gone.)

**How to read the numbers.** Ngram counts are per billion two-grams (or three-grams), averaged over 2018–2022, case-sensitive. "Total" is "feel X" + "feeling X" + "felt X", which is how rule 10 counts. Warriner valence runs from 1 to 9 with 5 as neutral.

## 1. The most important findings, ranked

### 1. Rule 6's list of cores contradicts rule 5, the research table, and Lazarus

*Confidence: high.*

Rule 6 lists seven cores, one question each: Fearful, Angry, Disgusted, Sad, Happy, Surprised, Bad. Rule 5 says a core is a sorting family or the Bad door, and that Fearful fails its naming test; the research table says the families are love, joy, anger, sadness, and fear, plus a small, pleasant surprise group, with disgust and contempt under anger. So rule 6's list:

- leaves out Love, a family in every sort except the Chinese one (with no Love question, Tender, Attracted, Close, and Devoted fall to Happy);
- lists Disgusted as a core, though it sorts under anger in all three languages;
- gives Surprised an unexpected-event question, which takes in unpleasant surprises (rule 1);
- uses Fearful as a core name.

Rule 6 also says "Each question follows the appraisal research: Richard Lazarus (1991)". Lazarus's 15 core relational themes have no theme for surprise and nothing like "Bad". They do have one for love ("desiring or participating in affection"). His anger theme is "a demeaning offense against me and mine", not "in your way"; goal blocking comes from other appraisal theorists.

**Fix:** rewrite rule 6's questions to match the cores rule 5 produces, add a Love question from Lazarus, fold "gross or wrong, want it away" into Angry's, and say the questions are adapted from appraisal theory (Lazarus 1991 and others), with no theme there for Surprise or Bad.

### 2. Rules 7 and 8 bring back the absolute "kind of" test the earlier refutation threw out

*Confidence: high.*

Rule 7's second paragraph ("The parent's meaning is true of every word under it") and rule 8's second bullet ("you can't feel terrified without feeling scared") are absolute. The research table says membership is fuzzy ("Test whether a word fits its parent better than anywhere else, not whether it fits perfectly"); report 4 found the absolute frame rejects about half the outer ring; Russell and Fehr found subcategories belong to anger "only by degree".

- **Cause words fail it by design.** "Feeling abandoned" reports a cause "which might be hurt, fear, or anger". You can feel betrayed and only furious, so no parent is true of Betrayed every time.
- **The Resentful example fails it too:** you can feel cheated and only foolish or sad.
- **Rule 7 disagrees with itself:** paragraph 1 is relative, paragraph 2 absolute, and "lower number wins" can't break a tie inside one rule.

**Fix:** keep paragraph 1. Reword paragraph 2: "The parent describes its children. Each child reads as the parent plus something specific (a cause, a target, or a strength), and most people who feel the child would also say they feel the parent. Cause words (rule 3) are exempt: they go under the feeling they most often bring." In rule 8, say "the outer word is a narrower case of the inner one", applied as a comparison between parents.

### 3. The Bad core can't be filled under rules 4–7

*Confidence: high.*

Rule 6 places words by the sorting studies first, and none has a Bad family. Every uncovered word whose situation can be named fails Bad's question ("something's off, and you can't say what"), and a specific feeling is one whose situation can be named. So rule 6 leaves Bad empty, while rule 5 requires 4 middle words. The words that fit Bad's question (Off, Weird, Out of sorts, Meh) are the ones rule 4 forbids. Report 4's check that Bad's children "don't all come from one other family" was dropped.

Of the 1,759 per billion "feel bad", "feel bad for" (346) and "feel bad about" (449) make up 45%: sympathy and guilt, which the rules place under Sad.

**Fix:** give Bad a question it can answer ("it's unpleasant, but it isn't loss, threat, or unfairness: too much, unsettled, out of sorts"), restore report 4's single-family check, and have Bad's card point to Guilty and Sorry for "I feel bad about …".

### 4. Rule 2 cuts Safe, Secure, Alone, Trapped, Stuck, and Helpless, and rule 3 can't save them

*Confidence: high.*

Rule 2 excludes "something happening around you (Busy, Rushed)"; rule 3 rescues only "what someone did to you". Ortony's objective descriptions include "alone, isolated, helpless, safe": your situation, not anyone's doing. They're among the most-used feeling phrases: Safe 2,910 per billion, Helpless 713, Alone 571, Secure 497, Trapped 424, Isolated 261, Stuck 213. Rushed is cut while Pressured, also something done to you, is kept.

**Fix:** widen rule 3 to "what happened to you, or the situation you're in", and narrow rule 2's bullet to "a schedule or workload (Busy, Rushed)".

### 5. Rule 10's check doesn't measure what it says, and its thresholds don't agree

*Confidence: high.*

The "feel ___" count doesn't count only feeling uses: "feel free to" is 90% of "feel free"; "feel sorry for" is 98% of "feel sorry"; "felt sure" (744) is certainty. And it misses words said with "be": "was worried" outnumbers "felt worried" 120:1, "was surprised" "felt surprised" 220:1, "was mad" 120:1, "in awe" "feel awe" about 200:1. The feel frame reverses Worried and Anxious (Anxious 4.7× in the feel frame; "was worried" 3.1× "was anxious").

Applied as written, Happy fails rule 5 (Good totals 5,545, 5.4× Happy), and Awe (21) and Fed up (7.8) look barely used.

The thresholds disagree: rule 10 says 5× and has no number for "barely used"; rule 5 says "the most common word … or one close to it"; report 4 proposed about 10×; report 5 used under 5 per billion with a flag under 15; report 4 had 50 raw SUBTLEX counts. "Plainer" isn't defined. And rule 10's first sentence makes use a condition for staying, while its last says commonness never removes a word that names something no other word does.

**Fix:** count each sense with a phrase that fixes it, compare by the larger of the be-frame and feel-frame totals, exclude non-feeling patterns, state one number per threshold, point rule 5 to them, and reword the first sentence: "A word stays if it names a feeling people talk about; commonness then only picks between synonyms."

### 6. Rule 11 removes "Down", the word rule 12 tells you to use

*Confidence: high.*

Down's usual meaning is a direction, and rule 11 outranks rule 12. The same reading removes Moved, Touched, Content, Blue, Low, Safe, Exposed, Cross, and Bad. "About yourself" is ambiguous: if the feeling must be about you, it cuts Compassion, Tenderness, Jealous, and Contempt.

**Fix:** "After 'I feel', the word's usual meaning is the feeling you mean (Down passes; Sensitive and Free don't). It describes the speaker's own state, not something mostly said of other people (Persecuted)."

### 7. Rule 3 has no test anyone can apply the same way twice

*Confidence: medium-high.*

The research table says Ortony's distinction isn't a test for one word at a time, and report 4 recommended a tagged list. Read literally, "what someone did to you" catches Hurt, Threatened (Ortony: affective), Embarrassed, Disappointed, Scared, Annoyed, and Humiliated, and every pleasant word for being treated well (Loved, Valued, Understood, Accepted, Forgiven). "Under the feeling it usually causes" names no data: Cheated could go to Resentful or Hurt, Ignored to Lonely, Hurt, or Angry.

**Fix:** a cause-word list (Ortony, Clore, and Foss's objective descriptions plus additions, each with a reason); its parent is the feeling its definition names, found by rule 6's question where no sort covers it.

### 8. The layout cap has no rank, its minimum may force filler, and "2 preferred" has no test

*Confidence: medium.*

The cap isn't numbered, so it can't be weighed against rule 13 when a common situation needs a fourth outer word. "2 or 3" also means never fewer than 2, which may force weak second words (Capable, Eager, Belittled, Discouraged: an inference). "A third word earns its place by … naming something no other word does" is the bar every word already meets under rule 8.

**Fix:** number the cap after rule 13; allow a middle word with no outer words; give the third word a real test or drop "preferred"; add an overflow step.

### 9. Rule 14 loses to rule 6 under the precedence rule, and its collapse test is too narrow

*Confidence: medium-high.*

Under "lower number wins", rule 6 (where people would look) overrides rule 14 (the English shape) in any language whose sorting differs, and the research shows they exist (Chinese shame, Basque shame near fear, Austroasiatic anxiety with grief). The test flags only neighboring words, so non-neighbors that share a translation (*avergonzado* for Ashamed and Embarrassed; *molesto* for Annoyed and Uncomfortable) slip through. Rules 1, 10, and 11 are English-only, and nothing says which rules a translation must pass.

**Fix:** "Rule 14 overrides rule 6 outside English"; flag any two positions with the same translation; translations keep the English word's valence sign in that language's norms where they exist.

### 10. Rule 1's lists rate how a word strikes people, not how the feeling feels

*Confidence: medium.*

Smug (3.68), Gloat (3.68), and Contempt (3.22) rate unpleasant though smugness feels good to the person feeling it; Blue (+6.53) and Cross (+5.67) pick up other senses; Bittersweet and Weird sit at 5.09; Surprised's SD of 2.20 suggests both kinds of surprise. The rule doesn't name its lists, gives no near-neutral band, and doesn't say where a failing word moves.

**Fix:** name the lists and the band; "a rating SD above 2.0 means the word is mixed or has more than one sense: write down which is meant"; a failing word moves to the family of its side that rule 6 picks.

### 11. Rule 6's Sad question misplaces shame-family words the studies don't cover

*Confidence: medium-high.*

Sad's question is about loss, but the sorting puts shame, guilt, embarrassment, insecurity, and loneliness under Sad. Uncovered words like Sheepish, Remorseful, Humbled, and Unworthy get matched to Fearful or Bad. Curious, Interested, and Intrigued match no question.

**Fix:** Sad: "you lost something or someone, fell short, did wrong, or were left out." Happy: "something good happened, things are going well, or something draws your interest."

### 12. The body wheel rule says to recheck but not what to check

*Confidence: medium.*

Report 4's invariants (2 or 3 feelings per sensation, no word with its parent or child) are gone, and nothing says what happens to a sensation that drops below 2 (cutting Tired and Apathetic empties Drained).

**Fix:** "Every sensation keeps 2–3 feelings that are on the feelings wheel, no two of them parent and child. A sensation that falls below 2 gets the nearest feeling by rule 6, or is removed with a note."

## 2. Rule by rule (findings not covered above)

- **Precedence:** rules 15 and 16 are procedures and never conflict on word choice, so "always apply" adds nothing for them.
- **Rule 0:** the research table's cores row ("Familiarity is the reason to keep them, not research") contradicts it. Rules 1, 5, and 6 lean on today's wheel in their examples. Nothing says whether passing the rules is enough to be included.
- **Rule 1:** "every list that has the word" closes report 4's missing-data hole. Startled (Warriner 4.53, SD 0.61) is near neutral, not plainly unpleasant. Pity (3.18) and Sympathy (6.57) split across families though Shaver sorts them together; the conflict isn't recorded.
- **Rule 2:** body-and-feeling words (Tense, Restless, Numb, Drained, Exhausted, Burned out) have no way to choose a reading; report 4's "rest or food would end it" test was dropped. "Just a way of treating other people" splits readers on Hostile and Defensive. Evaluative words (Awful, Terrible, Weird, Awkward, Worthless, Hopeless) are strictly "descriptions of a thing". Report 4's "judge words with a trait reading by their state reading" was dropped (Shy, Insecure, Confident, Anxious).
- **Rule 4:** the natural Bad words are the unspecific ones it bans. Removing Stressed as an umbrella holds.
- **Rule 5:** Love's most common word is Loved (371 vs Love's 215), a cause word. "Big" has no number, so whether pleasant surprise qualifies is undecidable. "A word small children know" names no data; report 4's "75% of 4–6-year-olds where Baron-Cohen lists it" was dropped. Verified: "feel afraid" is 6.9× "feel fearful" (5.8× across three frames); Scared (289) and Afraid (285) tie.
- **Rule 6:** "those piles tell us where most words go" overstates: the sort covered 135 words. The study-based placements match the reports.
- **Rule 7:** Jealous (fear and anger) has no parent whose meaning holds every time; "nervous before my wedding" isn't "something might hurt me". Name the association data (SWOW) and its polysemy bias.
- **Rule 8:** "side by side" is ambiguous; report 4's both-ways test for words in different families was dropped. The strength exception swallows the rule unless the test is "you can't feel A without feeling B". It doesn't say which sibling a stronger word goes under, or what happens to a middle word's own children if it moves.
- **Rule 9:** the example (Inferior under Insecure and Depressed) is stale: both homes are now in Sad, and Depressed is out under rule 12. Nothing says how to count each sense (Sorry for vs Sorry about; Proud of yourself vs of someone).
- **Rule 10:** book counts can't see slang (Pissed 12 in the feel frame vs "was pissed" 564; Bummed 3; Freaked out 2.3; Stoked 0.7), and the rules don't say whether slang is in.
- **Rule 12:** "medical" isn't defined (Anxious, Panicked, Traumatized, Triggered); Burned out is a condition by definition, so "describe a moment" needs saying for it.
- **Rule 13:** the list's source isn't named, nothing prevents the circularity report 5 found, one feeling per situation is wrong for mixed situations, and placement isn't checked. Proposed sources: Cowen and Keltner's 27 categories, Shaver's 135 words, Lazarus's 15 themes; 1–3 feelings per situation, each checked to be in the family rule 6 gives it.
- **Rule 14:** "neighboring" is undefined. "Most natural word" handles "tengo miedo".
- **Rule 16:** sound. NRC's terms ask for an About-page credit if used "in a product"; the rules should say whether using it only as a design check counts.
- **Layout cap:** nothing limits middle words per core.

## 3. Gaps

1. What earns the middle ring rather than the outer one.
2. Mixed and near-neutral feelings (Bittersweet, Ambivalent, Conflicted, Torn, Nostalgic, Awe, Hopeful).
3. Trait versus state readings.
4. When to split a word into two senses, and how to count each.
5. How definitions are written (a template: one sentence, second person, a moment; parent's feeling plus cause, target, or strength; cause words end by naming the feeling; no sibling's word).
6. Order within a ring.
7. What happens when a middle word would need a fourth outer word.
8. Inclusion and size: whether every word that passes must be added.
9. The body wheel's invariants.
10. What a core needs besides size: its own non-overlapping question, distinctness from other cores, and a card pointing to near misses.
11. Where the coverage list comes from.
12. Which rules a translation must pass.
13. Whether slang and spoken words are in.
14. Bad's "not all from one family" check.
