# Eisenberger and others: rejection hurts

The research behind giving rejection a body sensation in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md).

## What it is

- Naomi I. Eisenberger, Matthew D. Lieberman and Kipling D. Williams, "Does rejection hurt? An fMRI study of social exclusion", *Science* 302(5643), 290–292, 2003. [PubMed 14551436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14551436/).
- **The idea:** people were left out of a ball-tossing game in a brain scanner. Being excluded activated a brain area also active in physical pain, in step with how distressed people said they felt. Rejection is "social pain", and it registers like pain.
- **A caveat from later work:** Choong-Wan Woo and others, "Separate neural representations for physical pain and social rejection", *Nature Communications* 5, 5380, 2014, found that the brain area involved carries different patterns for the two. Rejection hurts, but it isn't the same signal as physical pain.
- Taken from the abstract and secondary coverage, read 2026-11-16; the full text wasn't read. The 2014 caveat was found by a fact-check on 2026-11-16 and not read either.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text or figures.

## Takeaways for this project
