# Which core would people tap first?

(Written on 2026-11-21 by a fresh research agent, saved here by the main session because the agent couldn't write files. Asked after Lucas set the north star, how people use the wheel: they start at the center and tap the core that matches what they feel. It looked for evidence on 17 words placed mostly by card-sorting. Disgust is in [research/9](9-disgust.md). Its scripts were in the session scratchpad and are gone. The figures it computed from raw data were re-run by a second agent; see the check at the end.)

## The two closest sources

No study was found of how people actually move through a feelings wheel. Two sources come closest to "which core would you tap first":

1. **Zupan, Dempsey, and Hartwell (2022, *Language and Cognition*),** from their raw data on OSF. Raters scored how well each word fits Happy, Sad, Angry, Fearful, and Neutral. The agent computed, for each word, the share of raters (about 30 per word) whose top score went to each category, splitting ties evenly: the closest thing to a "first tap". The options had no Love and no Bad (Neutral partly stands in for Bad), the raters were mostly women at a Canadian university, and it measures what a word means, not what people feel in the moment. A finding of its own: adding categories moved words. "Of the 24 words included in Study 2, only half were categorised using the same basic emotion categories as Study 1": guilty and overwhelmed moved from Sad to Anxiety, shocked from Fearful to Disgust. So whether there's a Bad core, and what's in it, shifts taps for borderline words.
2. **Trampe, Quoidbach, and Taquet (2015).** People recorded their feelings by phone through the day: 11,572 people, 65,721 reports. The agent computed correlations from the supplementary table S1. They're small because people tick few emotions per report, so compare them with each other, not with zero. Positive values in the table carry a light-gray minus sign; the agent read them as positive, which fit every case it could check (joy with satisfaction +.32).

Small World of Words associations (SWOW) were a weak extra signal.

## Per word

Zupan figures are the share of raters whose top score went to each category (Study 1), then the mean fit out of 9 for Happy / Sad / Angry / Fearful / Neutral where given; Study 2 offered 13 categories. Trampe figures are correlations from the daily reports. SWOW figures are association strengths.

- **Embarrassed.** Zupan: Fearful 46%, Sad 29%, Happy 9%, Angry 9%, Neutral 7% (1.97 / 5.13 / 4.40 / 5.50 / 2.63; low agreement). Trampe: guilt +.15, fear +.09, offense +.07, amusement +.07, gratitude +.06; sadness −.01, anger .00, anxiety −.01 (the paper calls embarrassment "distal": it rarely co-occurs). Leary 2015 (full text) pairs it with social anxiety. SWOW flat.
- **Ashamed.** Zupan ("shameful"): Sad 58%, Fearful 18%, Angry 16% (1.72 / 6.72 / 4.93 / 4.28). SWOW: ashamed → sad .067, fear .021, bad .018; shame → sad .047, bad .031, fear .031. Van Tilburg and Igou 2017 (preprint): after a film clip, shame with sadness .65, guilt .65, fear .59, anger .54; as a trait, with fear .63, sadness .52. Not among Trampe's emotions.
- **Guilty.** Zupan Study 1: Fearful 45%, Sad 36%, Angry 12% (1.38 / 6.45 / 5.34 / 6.03). Study 2 (13 options, n = 55): Anxiety 5.54, Fearful 4.98, Sad 3.63. Trampe: embarrassment +.15, fear +.11, offense +.09, sadness +.08. Van Tilburg: state guilt with fear .57, sadness .48. SWOW: guilty → bad .028, sad .011.
- **Insecure.** Zupan: Fearful 46%, Sad 37%, Angry 9% (1.69 / 6.76 / 4.59 / 6.72). SWOW: insecure → afraid .029, scared .026, sad .015; insecurity → fear .063, scared .037.
- **Lonely.** Zupan: Sad 75%, Neutral 10%, Fearful 8% (1.73 / 7.37 / 3.87 / 4.77). SWOW: lonely → sad .140.
- **Rejected.** Leary 2015 (full text): hurt feelings are "the rejection emotion"; in 168 hurtful episodes all but two involved feeling undervalued. Chow, Tiedens, and Govan 2008 (abstract): exclusion went with anger, unfair exclusion with more. SWOW: rejected → sad .057; rejection → sad .100. No Zupan data.
- **Betrayed.** Zupan: Angry 54%, Sad 32%, Fearful 11% (1.33 / 7.20 / 7.83 / 5.47). Sabini and Green 2004 (full text skimmed): sexual infidelity went with anger and blame, emotional infidelity with hurt. SWOW: betrayal → sad .019, anger .015.
- **Jealous.** Zupan: Angry 49%, Fearful 21%, Sad 16%, Neutral 13% (1.55 / 5.55 / 6.76 / 4.72). Parrott and Smith 1993 (abstract): "fear of loss, distrust, anxiety, and anger". SWOW: jealous → angry .042, anger .028, love .018, sad .018.
- **Envious.** Parrott and Smith 1993 (abstract): inferiority, longing, resentment, and disapproval of one's own feeling. SWOW: envious → bad .019; envy → bad .025, anger .007. No Zupan data.
- **Grateful.** Zupan (no Love option): Happy 91%; "thankful" 88%. Trampe: awe +.22, pride +.20, satisfaction +.17, hope +.15, joy +.15; love +.03. Algoe and Haidt 2009 (full text): in recall, 42% gave "happiness" as their best single word, 29% "gratitude"; love and gratitude formed one "warmth" factor (.91, .93). SWOW: grateful → happy .072, love .011.
- **Hatred.** Zupan ("hateful"): Angry 87% (1.13 / 3.93 / 8.33 / 4.30). SWOW: hatred → anger .125. Martínez, van Prooijen, and Van Lange 2022 (abstract; 1,074 people): hate is more arousing, threatening, and attack-oriented than anger, and conceptually closer to disgust and contempt.
- **Shocked.** Zupan Study 1: Fearful 32%, Angry 24%, Neutral 21%, Happy 14%, Sad 8% (very low agreement). Study 2: Disgust 3.81, Fearful 3.48, Anger 3.30, Anxiety 3.15. "Stunned": Neutral 38%, Fearful 26%. "Surprised": Happy 71%. SWOW: shocked → scared .028.
- **Nostalgic.** Newman, Sachs, Stone, and Schwarz 2020 (full text): in daily diaries nostalgia went with sad, low feelings (.20), tense feelings (.13), and loneliness (.17), not upbeat ones (.00); in the moment, only with sad, low feelings (.16). Recalled nostalgic experiences were more positive than nostalgic diary days (5.95 vs 5.02). Sedikides et al. 2008 (full text): recalled nostalgia "induced higher levels of happiness than of sadness". SWOW: nostalgia → sad .018, happy .011.
- **Bored.** Zupan: Neutral 76%, Sad 16%. Chin et al. 2017 (full text; 1.1 million half-hour reports from 3,867 adults): when bored vs not, happiness .06 vs .30, contentedness .12 vs .45, loneliness .05 vs .01, anger .03 vs .01, sadness .02 vs .01. Van Tilburg and Igou 2017: induced boredom correlated with none of 10 negative emotions (sadness −.01, anger .06, fear .12).
- **Confused.** Zupan: Angry 29%, Fearful 27%, Neutral 21%, Sad 20%. Balaž et al. 2022 (full text; 545 students): confusion with anxiety .70, frustration .65, boredom .57.
- **Overwhelmed.** Zupan Study 1: Sad 35%, Fearful 31%, Neutral 19%, Angry 12%. Study 2: Anxiety 7.94, Fearful 5.09, Sad 4.43, Irritated 4.02, Anger 3.65. A 2025 study of 94 workplace accounts (via a summary tool): stress 45%, frustration and anger 30%, anxiety 30%, panic 22%.
- **Hurt.** Zupan Study 1: Sad 63%, Angry 23%, Fearful 11%. Study 2: Sad 7.56, Anger 5.65, Anxiety 4.15. Leary et al. 1998: hurt is "undifferentiated negative affect", often with anxiety and hostility.

## Words where people would split

- **Betrayed:** about 60/40 toward Angry over Sad.
- **Guilty:** close to even between Scared and Sad, with Anxiety highest when offered; Bad may draw it too ("bad" is its top association).
- **Insecure:** about 55/45 toward Scared.
- **Embarrassed:** Scared 46%, Sad 29%; daily reports don't support Sad at all.
- **Overwhelmed:** Scared, Sad, and Angry, leaning Scared.
- **Jealous:** about 2 to 1 Angry over Scared.
- **Hurt:** about 3 to 1 Sad.
- **Nostalgic:** Sad in the moment, Happy for cherished memories.
- **Grateful:** Happy over Love about 5 to 1 in daily reports and associations; no forced choice offered Love.
- **Shocked:** three ways.
- **Confused:** four ways, which supports a catch-all.
- **Envious:** no data.

## Not obtained

Cowen and Keltner 2017 (corrupt PDF); Hepper et al. 2012; Parrott and Smith's item-level numbers; Tangney et al. 1996 (secondary only); Sharpsteen, Fitness, and Fletcher 1993; co-occurrence data from Smith and Ellsworth, Roseman, Frijda, and Kuppens. ## The check

A second agent re-derived the figures from the downloaded data on 2026-11-22, with its own parsers.

- **Zupan et al.:** every share and mean confirmed (29 or 30 raters per word; the recomputed means match the paper's Appendix A to two decimals), and so are the Study 2 means and the "only half of 24 words" sentence. Caveat: ties at the top are common (19 of 30 raters for Betrayed; 13 for Guilty and Overwhelmed). Counting only raters with a single top choice gives the same leader for every word, but for **Guilty and Insecure** the share and the mean disagree: the paper's own method, highest mean, puts both under Sad (Guilty 6.45 vs 6.03; Insecure 6.76 vs 6.72), and rater by rater Fearful wins only 11 to 8 and 10 to 9. Treat both as ties.
- **Trampe et al.:** every correlation confirmed. The hidden minus is white on unshaded cells and light gray on shaded ones; reading those cells as positive fits every check. Real negatives show a visible minus.
- **Newman et al. 2020:** confirmed; the figures are within-person multilevel coefficients, not correlations.
- **Algoe and Haidt 2009:** confirmed, but the .91 and .93 "warmth" loadings are from Study 2a, whose recalled events were elevation, admiration, and amusement, not gratitude (Study 2b: .78 and .75). So nothing here supports Love as Grateful's second door: in daily reports, love (.03) ranks below awe, pride, satisfaction, hope, and joy.
- **Chin et al. 2017:** confirmed; the shares are from the 707,431 reports by the 2,447 people who were ever bored.

**What stands:** Ashamed, Lonely, Betrayed (leaning Angry, about 63/37), Jealous, Hatred, Bored, Confused, Hurt, Nostalgic, and Shocked as written; Embarrassed toward Scared holds by share, mean, and daily reports. Guilty and Insecure are ties between Scared and Sad. Grateful points to Happy, with no evidence for Love second.
