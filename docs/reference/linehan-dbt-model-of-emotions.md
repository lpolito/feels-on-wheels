# Marsha Linehan: DBT's model of emotions

The research behind the kinds of urge in the body wheel's **Urges** area in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md): in dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), body sensations and action urges are two separate parts of an emotion.

## What it is

- Marsha M. Linehan, *DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets*, 2nd ed., Guilford Press, 2015 ([publisher](https://www.guilford.com/books/DBT-Skills-Training-Handouts-and-Worksheets/Marsha-Linehan/9781572307810)). Emotion Regulation Handout 5, "A model for describing emotions", with Worksheet 4.
- **The model:** an emotion is a chain from a prompting event and how you interpret it, through **body changes and sensations** and **action urges**, to what you show and do, and its aftereffects. Changing any link can change the rest. A later handout lists the usual sensations and urges for each of several emotions.
- **Opposite action**, a DBT skill, starts from naming the urge and, when the emotion doesn't fit the facts, doing the opposite.
- **Radical acceptance**, a DBT distress tolerance skill, is accepting reality as it is, completely and without fighting it. It isn't approval, passivity, or resignation. Taken from secondary sources that quote Linehan's handouts, such as [Skyland Trail's](https://skylandtrail.org/?p=2629), read 2026-11-16.
- Taken from secondary summaries, including a [Kaiser Permanente class handout](https://mydoctor.kaiserpermanente.org/ncal/Images/Emotion%20Regulation%20DBT%20Skills%20ADA%2004292020_tcm75-1598999.pdf), read 2026-11-16; the book itself wasn't read.

## Usage terms

Copyrighted handouts, widely used in therapy. We use the model, not its handouts or word lists.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Sensations and urges are different parts of an emotion,** which backs keeping Urges as its own area instead of mixing urges in with the places.
2. **Each emotion comes with its own urges,** including ones Frijda's list doesn't stress: guilt pulls toward making it right, sadness toward pulling back, shame toward hiding, jealousy toward controlling others, and anger toward attacking. The last two were confirmed in opposite-action handouts based on DBT, such as [Choosing Therapy's](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Opposite-Action-Examples-Handout.pdf), read 2026-11-16, not in Linehan's own handout.
3. **Accepting your situation is a recognized state,** not just giving up, which backs Content and At peace under the body wheel's Nothing much.
4. **Naming an urge is a skill people are taught,** so an urge is a fair way into a feeling for someone who notices the pull first.
