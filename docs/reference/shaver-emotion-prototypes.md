# Shaver and others: how people sort feeling words

Why the [opinionated feelings wheel](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-26-feels-on-wheels-opinionated-wheel-design.md) has the families it has: love, joy, anger, sadness, and fear, plus a catch-all.

## What it is

- Phillip Shaver, Judith Schwartz, Donald Kirson and Cary O'Connor, "Emotion knowledge: Further exploration of a prototype approach", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 52(6), 1061–1086, 1987. [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.52.6.1061](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.52.6.1061).
- **What they did:** students rated 213 emotion words for how good an example of an emotion each was; another 100 sorted the top 135 into groups, analyzed by cluster analysis.
- **What they found:** above everything, a split between pleasant and unpleasant. Below it, five families: love, joy, anger, sadness, and fear, plus a very small surprise group on the pleasant side. Disgust, contempt, envy, and jealousy sorted under anger; shame, guilt, embarrassment, humiliation, insecurity, rejection, and loneliness under sadness.
- **W. Gerrod Parrott,** *Emotions in Social Psychology*, 2001, reformats this tree into the table most feelings-word lists copy. It isn't independent evidence.
- **Replications:** Shaver, Wu and Schwartz 1992 (United States, Italy, China: in China love wasn't its own family, and shame was); Shaver, Murdaya and Fraley 2001 (Indonesia: the same five families, no surprise group); Alonso-Arbiol, Shaver and others 2006 (Basque: the five families, with surprise words often sorting with fear). In all three, disgust and contempt fell under anger.
- The 1987 paper wasn't reached; its method and findings were confirmed through the Basque replication, which was read in full, and secondary sources (research for the opinionated wheel, 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-22).

## Usage terms

Published research. We use its findings, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Each core is all pleasant or all unpleasant,** since that's the first split.
2. **The families are evidence of which words seem alike,** which informs which core someone would tap. They don't decide it: sorting cards isn't looking for your own feeling from the center. Disgust sorts under anger, but physical disgust isn't anger to the person feeling it, so the wheel puts it under Bad.
3. **Love is a core.** People sort it as a family of its own, though it has no single face.
