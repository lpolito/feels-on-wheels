# Alan Marlatt: urge surfing

The research behind treating an urge as something you can notice, in the body wheel's **Urges** area in the [body wheel rework](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-feels-on-wheels-body-wheel-rework-design.md).

## What it is

- G. Alan Marlatt (1941–2011), a psychologist at the University of Washington, known for relapse prevention in addiction treatment. Urge surfing comes from G. Alan Marlatt and Judith R. Gordon, eds., *Relapse Prevention*, Guilford Press, 1985.
- Tested in: Sarah Bowen and Alan Marlatt, "Surfing the urge: Brief mindfulness-based intervention for college student smokers", *Psychology of Addictive Behaviors* 23(4), 666–671, 2009. [doi:10.1037/a0017127](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017127).
- **The idea:** an urge rises, peaks and passes like a wave. Noticing it, including where it sits in the body, instead of acting on it or fighting it, is a skill that helps people in recovery.
- Taken from secondary summaries, read 2026-11-16; neither the book nor the paper was read, and the 2009 study's results weren't checked.

## Usage terms

Published research. We use the idea, not its text.

## Takeaways for this project

1. **Noticing an urge is a recovery skill,** so naming urges such as wanting to numb out belongs on a wheel for finding what you feel.
2. **The urge is not the feeling.** Naming the urge first and then the feeling under it is the order many people find them in.
